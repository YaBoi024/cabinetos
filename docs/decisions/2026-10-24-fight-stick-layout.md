# A fight stick the console knows gets an arcade layout

- **Decided:** The 8BitDo Arcade Stick, over Bluetooth in X mode, plays FBNeo and MAME 2003-Plus games with one fixed layout, a Vewlix panel's: the game's buttons in the game's own order, the top row then the bottom, light to heavy left to right where the game numbers them so. A game that numbers its punches first (Street Fighter's six) gets punches on top and kicks below; one that does not follows its own numbering (MAME 2003-Plus numbers Mortal Kombat II's Low Punch, Low Kick, High Punch, High Kick, Block, Block 2). Four buttons take the bottom row, as on a Neo Geo cabinet's one row; one to three take the bottom row too, with a gap before the third. FBNeo's own combination buttons (A+B, 3x Punch and the like) get no button on the stick. Its Home opens the pause menu in every game. Every other pad, and the stick in every other game, plays exactly as before: no setting, no screen. A stick is known only from a table of sticks measured on a console, never guessed. The 8BitDo Arcade Stick is officially supported (MMagTech, after the TV run).
- **Date:** 2026-10-24
- **Why:** A stick's eight buttons are a cabinet's panel; read by position like a pad's, a game's buttons land wherever its RetroPad ids fall, not in the cabinet's rows. Nobody without a stick may notice anything, so the rule keys on the stick itself. Over Bluetooth in X mode the stick carries the Xbox One S pad's own id, 045e:02e0, and SDL calls it an Xbox One S Controller; only the kernel's name for its device, "8BitDo Arcade Stick", tells it apart, so the table matches the id and that name, never SDL's. Measured on the A9 and rejected as ways to know it: on a cable in X mode it is a plain 045e:028e "Microsoft X-Box 360 pad" (maker "8BitDo", product "Controller"), the same as other pads; in S mode it is a Switch Pro Controller to everything, and the kernel could not start it over USB; its 2.4G receiver, 2dc8:3207 "8BitDo Arcade Stick Receiver", was not measured with the stick on it. In those it plays as a pad. A stick has no stick clicks, so the pause pair cannot be pressed, and a tap of Home is the way in, as on a stream. MAME 2003-Plus declares a game's buttons in RetroPad id order, so their order comes from its Classic table at our pin; FBNeo declares them in the game's order. At the TV the four-button square felt wrong and the bottom row "way better" (MMagTech, Metal Slug and Shock Troopers); the combination buttons, which FBNeo adds and no cabinet had, "feel like cheating", and on a stick two buttons are pressed together as on the cabinet. FBNeo names them "Buttons ...", the one label the console reads.
