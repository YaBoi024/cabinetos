# LinkUp's picture never goes below 720p at 3.5 Mbit/s

- **Decided:** When the connection cannot keep up, the host lowers the bitrate first, from 18 to 12 to 8 Mbit/s at its own size, then a 1080p host drops to 720p at 6 and then 3.5 Mbit/s. 720p at 3.5 Mbit/s is the lowest step, and a host below 1080p keeps its size and only lowers the bitrate. Below that nothing smaller is tried: after 6 seconds at the lowest step still losing, both consoles say *Slow connection* and the game carries on.
- **Date:** 2026-11-07
- **Why:** A friend watches on a TV. Below 720p the picture is soft enough to look broken from across the room and a game's small text is hard to read, and no TV or monitor a player would use is smaller. A connection that cannot carry 720p at 3.5 Mbit/s is the network's fault, a smaller picture does not fix the lag that comes with it, and LinkUp is not built around one: the console says so plainly. The options were steps at 540p and below, as other streaming apps have, and one more step at 720p at 2.5 Mbit/s, which needs no new encoder but is blockier; both help only a host whose upload is under about 3.5 Mbit/s, and each change of size is a hitch while a fresh full picture is sent. The reference console's video engine hung at other changes of size (docs/lessons/frontend.md, "LinkUp's stream"), but that is not the reason, and the floor stays if that hang is fixed. A server's owner learns from its setup guide what a relayed game costs; a player needs only the notice.
