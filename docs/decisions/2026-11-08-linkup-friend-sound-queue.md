# A LinkUp friend plays the sound from its own queue, at the host's pace

- **Decided:** The friend's console takes the decoded sound into a queue of LinkUp's own and plays it through a PipeWire stream, trimming PipeWire's resampler by at most 2000 ppm either way so the queue stays at its target. The target is 40 ms, grows by 20 ms after each Wi-Fi stall the queue did not cover, up to 100 ms, and shrinks by 10 ms after each two calm minutes. The program is `linkup/friend_sound.cpp`.
- **Date:** 2026-11-08
- **Why:** Two consoles' sound clocks never match: the mini PC sent 47,960.6 samples a second by the reference console's clock, so a queue played at the friend's own pace ran dry within a minute and crackled from then on. GStreamer's sinks could not be made to follow the host: pulsesink and pipewiresink with a queue ran dry the same way, a sink playing by timestamps took every piece as late in a pipeline built while it runs, and pipewire-pulse reports 0 for its queue's delay, so nothing could steer by it. Dropping or repeating samples, as Moonlight does, is heard. Trimming a resampler from a queue the program measures exactly is what WebRTC's own player and PipeWire's RTP receiver do. 40 ms is Moonlight's range; Wi-Fi on the reference console delivered pieces 50 to 80 ms late now and then, which a fixed 40 ms cannot cover, so the target grows only where a network needs it. 100 ms is the most because sound trailing the picture is noticed from about 125 ms (ITU-R BT.1359). A queue dry for longer than 300 ms is the host gone quiet (a menu, a pause), not the network, and grows nothing.
- **Numbers:** #654, "LinkUp: play a game with a friend on another CabinetOS console over the internet"; PR #666, "LinkUp: the console's side, step 3 of #654".
- **Replaces:** none.
