Deliberately not derived from MAME
----------------------------------

Two commodity parts are implemented from their published programming models
rather than ported, because they are fully documented general-purpose chips: no
reverse-engineering insight is embodied in one implementation over another, so
there is nothing to be gained by tracking MAME's, and MAME's versions are woven
into its device framework in ways a port would have to unpick.

  Motorola 68000 (the sound board CPU)
    Musashi, by Karl Stenerud, used unmodified and configured through compile
    definitions. MIT licence. https://github.com/kstenerud/Musashi
    MAME's own 68000 is either generated from a table into some two hundred
    thousand lines per variant, or is itself a fork of Musashi.

  Intel 8251A / NEC uPD71051 USART (the sound command link)
    src/hw/i8251.cpp, i8251.h — written for this project.
    MAME's i8251 is bit-serial through device_serial_interface, and so is the
    SCSP at the other end of the wire; this models whole bytes and keeps only the
    byte period, which is the part the sound driver's pacing depends on.

Architectural influence
-----------------------

The application structure — the XML-driven ROM database with content-addressed
CRC32 identification and stride/chunk interleaving, the snapshot-based handoff
between emulation and rendering, and the layered input abstraction — was
informed by the design of Supermodel, the Sega Model 3 emulator
(https://supermodel3.com). Supermodel is licensed under the GPL; no Supermodel
code is included in sm2-emu except where contributed directly by its original
author under the terms of this project's licence.
